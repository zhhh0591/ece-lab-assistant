# 2026-09-29

I uploaded my MaUWB_DW3000 self-test tools to this repo: the Arduino self-test, a flash script, and the Python scripts that read and log the results. They still need work because I need to add range test into the selftest.py and right now it only works on one board. It can only reflect the board and be open but can't determine the range.
So I studied a How To Electronics video and its written tutorial on range testing this board, to prepare for my own ranging test. What I learned:

- UWB sends very short pulses spread over 500 MHz or more. A sharp pulse gives a
  precise arrival time. This board uses channel 5, 6489.6 MHz.
- Distance comes from two-way ranging. The tag sends a poll and the anchor
  answers after a reply delay. Time of flight is (round trip - reply delay) / 2,
  and 1 ns is about 30 cm.
- The STM32 firmware does the ranging. The ESP32 only sends AT commands over
  UART at 115200 and reads the results.
- Setup is AT+RESTORE, then AT+SETCFG=id,role,rate,filter (role 0 is tag, 1 is
  anchor; rate 0 is 850 kbps, 1 is 6.8 Mbps), AT+SETCAP=32,15 for 32 tags and a
  15 ms slot, AT+SETRPT=1 to report automatically, then AT+SAVE and AT+RESTART.
- Results come back as AT+RANGE lines with range in cm and RSSI in dBm. RSSI is
  logarithmic, so 10 dB lower is one tenth the power.
- The antenna is directional, about 4.16 dBi. Boards should face each other and
  sit 1.2 to 1.5 m off the ground.

The pins in the video's code match my self-test for this board.

In the video, two boards 50 cm apart read 48 to 51 cm, and outdoors the range
reached about 480 m. RSSI stayed at -90 from 200 to 351 m, which doesn't look
like a real measurement, so I won't treat RSSI as a precise value.
