About the lab

The ECE Makerspace & Capstone Laboratory is where Electrical and Computer Engineering capstone teams design, build and test their projects.

See the department's ECE Capstone page for the course itself.

Some famous people in the field

Sir Isaac Newton (1643–1727)
His development of calculus, independently of Leibniz, gave engineers the mathematics they use to describe and solve electrical problems.
Michael Faraday (1791–1867)
Discovered electromagnetic induction and made key contributions to the understanding of electricity and magnetism.
James Clerk Maxwell (1831–1879)
Formulated the theory of electromagnetism, summed up in Maxwell's equations.
Nikola Tesla (1856–1943)
Pioneered alternating current (AC) power transmission, and invented the induction motor and the Tesla coil.
Guglielmo Marconi (1874–1937)
Showed that radio waves could carry signals without wires, laying the foundation for modern wireless communication.
Hedy Lamarr (1914–2000)
Co-patented a frequency-hopping spread spectrum system, an idea behind later wireless systems including Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and cellular networks.
Katherine Johnson (1918–2020)
Calculated by hand the trajectories, launch windows and emergency return paths for NASA missions, including John Glenn's orbital flight in 1962.

About this website

The site is plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, served by the lab's Apache server. It follows the Northeastern brand: the university's global header and footer, Lato and Playfair Display type, and Northeastern red as an accent. It aims to meet WCAG 2.1 AA, as the Northeastern brand guidelines ask.

Pages are generated from a few data files (equipment, FAQ, hours, documents) by a short Python script, so updating the lab's hours or adding an instrument means editing one file. The source and instructions are kept by the lab staff.
